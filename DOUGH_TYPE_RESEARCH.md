# DOUGH TYPE RESEARCH AND INFORMATION

This document provides research-based information about various bread dough types and their characteristics for accurate calculations.

## Basic White Bread

**Characteristics:**
- Simple flour, water, salt, and yeast combination
- Light texture with mild flavor
- Easy to make, forgiving for beginners
- High gluten development through proper kneading
- Typical hydration: 60-65%

**Uses:**
- Everyday breads like sandwich loaves
- French bread, dinner rolls
- Base for other enriched doughs

## Enriched Dough (Brioche)

**Characteristics:**
- Rich, tender dough with eggs and butter for exceptional softness
- Higher fat content due to enriching ingredients
- Creates a very soft, almost cake-like texture
- Requires more careful handling due to rich ingredients
- Effective hydration accounts for water from eggs, milk, and butter

**Uses:**
- Brioche breads
- French toast or pancakes
- Dessert breads with custard or fruit

## Sweet Dough (Challah)

**Characteristics:**
- Traditional braided bread using eggs and oil
- Sweet but not overly so - natural sweetness from ingredients
- Creates an attractive braided appearance
- Soft, tender texture that's perfect for braiding
- The water content from eggs is factored into hydration calculations

**Uses:**
- Jewish braided breads
- Breakfast rolls
- Dessert breads

## Whole Wheat Bread

**Characteristics:**
- Nutritious dough using whole wheat flour
- Higher fiber content than white flour bread
- Denser crumb than white flour equivalents
- Requires more water due to absorption properties of whole grains
- Higher hydration needed: 70-75%

**Uses:**
- Health-conscious baking
- Sandwich bread alternatives
- Rustic loaves

## Rye Bread

**Characteristics:**
- Distinctive tangy flavor from rye flour
- Dense texture with a characteristic crumb structure
- High absorption properties of rye flour require higher hydration
- Often uses a mix of rye and wheat flours
- Requires longer fermentation times

**Uses:**
- Traditional rye breads (like pumpernickel)
- Potentially dense but flavorful breads
- Sandwich bread alternatives

## Italian Bread (Ciabatta, Pullman)

**Characteristics:**
- Light, airy crumb with crispy exterior
- High hydration levels (60-70%)
- Creates characteristic open air pockets
- Often uses a preferment method for complex flavor
- The water content from any oil included is factored into hydration

**Uses:**
- Sandwich breads
- Bruschetta or appetizer accompaniments
- Fresh crusty breads

## Milk Bread

**Characteristics:**
- Soft, sweet, tender crumb with golden color from milk
- Classic enriched bread recipe
- The water content from milk is factored into hydration calculations  
- Smooth, velvety texture
- Light, sweet flavor that's perfect for snacking

**Uses:**
- Dinner rolls
- Soft sandwich bread
- Breakfast toast

## Sourdough Bread

**Characteristics:**
- Fermented dough using naturally cultivated starter
- Complex, tangy flavors over time
- Better shelf life due to fermentation process
- Requires time and care for starter maintenance
- No commercial yeast needed - the starter provides leavening

**Uses:**
- Traditional sourdough breads
- Artisan breads
- Bread with complex flavor profiles and textures

## Hydration Calculations

The calculator accounts for effective hydration by determining how much water is contributed by enriching ingredients:

- **Eggs**: 75% water content (1 egg ≈ 40g water)
- **Milk**: 87% water content (1 cup ≈ 238g water)  
- **Butter**: 16% water content (1 cup ≈ 238g water)
- **Cream**: 57.5% average water content

This ensures accurate hydration calculations that reflect the true effective water content of your dough.

## Bread Flour Variations

The following flours commonly used:
- All-purpose flour: Good for most breads, medium protein content
- Bread flour: High protein content, good gluten development  
- Whole wheat flour: Nutritious but requires more water
- Rye flour: More absorbent than wheat flour, higher hydration needed
