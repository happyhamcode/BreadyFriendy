Understanding Hydration in Bread Making

Hydration refers to the amount of water in a dough, expressed as a percentage of the flour weight. It's a crucial factor that determines the texture, structure, and handling characteristics of your bread.

How Hydration Affects Bread

  • Low Hydration (50-60%): Dense, tight crumb; easier to handle, good for structured breads
  • Medium Hydration (60-70%): Balanced texture with good structure and slight openness
  • High Hydration (70%+): Open crumb with air pockets; requires more technique for handling

Different Hydration Ranges and Their Results

50-60%: Dense loaf, tight crumb - often used in sandwich breads and structured loaves

60-65%: Standard bread texture - good all-purpose hydration level

65-70%: Light, airy crumb with a more open structure - great for rustic breads

70-75%: Very open crumb, soft and tender - typical for certain artisan breads

75%+: Extremely airy crumb with large holes - often used in pizza dough or very wet doughs

Why Understanding Hydration Matters

The hydration level you choose will affect how your bread behaves during mixing, proofing, and baking. Higher hydration doughs are more challenging to handle but produce more open crumb structures that many bakers love. Lower hydration is easier to work with but results in denser textures.

When using enriched doughs (containing eggs, milk, butter, etc.), the water content from these ingredients should be accounted for in your effective hydration calculation to achieve the desired final texture.
