Basic White Bread

A simple yet perfect recipe using just flour, water, salt, and yeast. Ideal for everyday loaves with a light texture.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides gluten structure for the bread
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates yeast
  • Salt: Enhances flavor and controls fermentation
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise through fermentation

Baking Tips

Basic white bread benefits from a long, slow first rise for better gluten development. The light texture comes from proper kneading and gentle handling during proofing.

Enriched Dough (Brioche)

Rich, tender dough with eggs and butter for exceptional softness and flavor. Perfect for enriched breads.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides structure
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates yeast (water from eggs and milk is already accounted for)
  • Eggs: Add richness, tenderness, and a golden color
  • Milk: Provides moisture and adds flavor
  • Butter: Adds richness and creates a tender crumb
  • Salt: Enhances the natural sweetness and controls fermentation
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise

Baking Tips

Brioche requires gentle handling as the high fat content makes it more delicate. A long, cool first rise helps develop flavor while maintaining tenderness.

Sweet Dough (Challah)

Traditional braided bread using eggs and oil for distinctive flavor and texture. Sweet but not overly so.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides the structure
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates yeast (water from eggs is already accounted for)
  • Eggs: Add richness, tenderness, and a golden color
  • Oil: Creates a soft, tender texture
  • Salt: Enhances and balances the natural sweetness
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise

Baking Tips

Challah benefits from a warm, humid proofing environment for best braiding. The oil content gives the bread its characteristic soft texture.

Whole Wheat Bread

Nutritious dough using whole wheat flour with optional enrichments. Higher fiber content and robust flavor.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Whole Wheat Flour: Provides nutrients, fiber, and nutty flavor
  • Water: Hydrates the flour and activates yeast
  • Salt: Enhances flavor and controls fermentation
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise
  • Optional enrichments (eggs, milk, butter): Add tenderness and moisture when included

Baking Tips

Whole wheat bread benefits from a longer fermentation time to develop flavor. The whole grains absorb more water, so increase hydration as needed.

Rye Bread

Distinctive tangy flavor using rye flour with possible enrichments. Higher hydration due to rye's absorption properties.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Rye Flour: Provides the distinctive tangy flavor and dense texture
  • Water: Hydrates the flour and activates yeast
  • Salt: Enhances flavor and controls fermentation
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise (rye often requires a longer fermentation)
  • Optional enrichments (eggs, milk, butter): Add richness when included

Baking Tips

Rye bread benefits from a long fermentation time and often needs more water than wheat bread due to rye's high absorption properties. Consider adding vital wheat gluten for better structure.

Italian Bread (Ciabatta, Pullman)

Light, airy crumb with crispy exterior. Great for sandwiches and traditional Italian breads.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides the structure
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates yeast
  • Salt: Enhances flavor and controls fermentation
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise
  • Optional oil: Adds tenderness to the crumb

Baking Tips

Italian breads benefit from a high hydration recipe and proper fermentation. Techniques like stretch and folds can develop the characteristic airiness. A hot oven with steam creates the signature crispy crust.

Milk Bread

Soft, sweet, tender crumb with golden color from milk. Classic enriched bread.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides the structure
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates yeast (water from milk is already accounted for)
  • Milk: Adds moisture, sugar, and creates a golden color
  • Butter: Adds richness and creates tenderness
  • Salt: Enhances flavor
  • Yeast: Makes the dough rise

Baking Tips

Milk bread is best when baked in a preheated oven for a golden, slightly crispy crust. The milk content contributes to both the sweet flavor and tender crumb.

Sourdough Bread

Fermented dough using a starter for complex flavor and texture. Develops unique taste over time.

What Goes Into This Bread?

  • Flour: Provides structure
  • Water: Hydrates flour and activates fermentation
  • Salt: Enhances flavor and controls fermentation
  • Sourdough Starter: Natural fermentation agent providing both leavening and complex flavors

Baking Tips

Sourdough requires maintaining a healthy starter with regular feeding. Proper timing of fermentation is crucial - over-proofing will result in sour flavor, under-proofing will give dense results.
