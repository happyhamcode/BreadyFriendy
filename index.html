What is hydration?

Hydration is the weight of water in a dough as a percentage of the weight of flour. Every ingredient is measured in grams, because a cup of flour can vary by 30 g or more between bakers.

Example: 500 g flour and 325 g water is 325 ÷ 500 = 65% hydration.

  • Under 45%Too dry for most bread. Closer to pasta or cracker dough.
  • 45-55%Brioche, bagels, pretzels, croissant dough.
  • 55-62%Challah, cinnamon rolls, Neapolitan-style pizza.
  • 62-70%Sandwich loaves, dinner rolls, milk bread, New York-style pizza, classic French bread.
  • 70-80%Rustic baguettes, country loaves, Hokkaido milk bread, pan pizza.
  • 80-90%Ciabatta, focaccia, Roman-style pizza.
  • 90%+Pan de cristal and no-knead batters. Not a kneaded dough.

Rich doughs read low: butter and eggs add richness, not water, so brioche is about 45-55% true hydration.

What is enriched dough?

Enriched dough is yeast dough with fat, sugar, eggs or dairy added to the flour, water, yeast and salt. Brioche, challah, milk bread, cinnamon rolls and dinner rolls are enriched. The result is a softer, richer crumb that stays fresh longer.

Here is the catch: many enrichers are partly water. Milk is about 88% water, a large egg about 76%, butter about 16%. Bread Friend counts that hidden water, so the hydration you see is the true hydration of your dough.

Leave something blank and Bread Friend fills in the recommended amount: 65% hydration, 2% salt, 1% instant yeast (1.3% active dry, 3% fresh), and the enricher amounts on each card below.

What each enricher does

Whole egg

Does: adds protein, fat and water for structure, richness and golden color. 50 g per large egg.

Use when: you want a rich, bread-like dough: challah, brioche, soft rolls. Typical: 10-25% of flour weight.

Left blank, we use 15% of flour weight.

Egg yolk

Does: more fat and emulsifiers, less water than whole egg. Softer, more tender, deeper yellow. 14 g per yolk.

Use when: you want a cake-like crumb. Typical: 5-15% of flour weight.

Left blank, we use 10% of flour weight.

Milk

Does: replaces some or all of the water. Milk sugar (lactose) browns the crust and the proteins soften the crumb.

Use when: making milk bread, sandwich loaves and rolls. Typical: replaces half to all of the water.

Left blank, we use half of the water as milk.

Heavy cream

Does: brings liquid and fat together for a tender, rich crumb.

Use when: you want richness without handling butter. Typical: replaces part of the liquid.

Left blank, we use a quarter of the water as cream.

Butter

Does: coats gluten strands so the crumb is tender, adds flavor, and slows staling. One US stick is 113 g.

Use when: you want classic rich flavor. Typical: 8-15% for rolls, 25-50% for brioche. Add it after the gluten has formed.

Left blank, we use 12% of flour weight. Go to 25-50% for brioche.

Oil

Does: pure fat, so no water. A soft crumb that stays moist, and the dough mixes easily.

Use when: you want softness without butter flavor, or a dairy-free dough. Typical: 4-10%.

Left blank, we use 7% of flour weight.

Sugar

Does: tenderizes, browns the crust and holds moisture. Yeast also feeds on it, but a lot of sugar draws water away from yeast and slows rising.

Use when: sweet doughs. Typical: 5-15%. Above 25%, use osmotolerant yeast.

Left blank, we use 10% of flour weight.

Honey

Does: sweetness, floral flavor and moisture retention. It is partly water, so it counts toward hydration.

Use when: whole-grain or rustic enriched loaves. Typical: 5-10%.

Left blank, we use 7% of flour weight.

Milk powder

Does: adds milk proteins and sugar with almost no water. Softer crumb and deeper crust color without making the dough wetter.

Use when: you want a milky flavor but need to keep hydration low. Typical: 3-6%.

Left blank, we use 4% of flour weight.
