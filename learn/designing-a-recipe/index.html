Designing an Enriched Dough Recipe

You do not need a recipe to start. You need a target and a few choices, and the calculator fills in the rest.

Five steps

  1. Pick a target hydration. Using King Arthur's recipes as a guide: challah is about 50%. Brioche and Neapolitan-style pizza are 55 to 62%. Sandwich bread, bagels and pretzels are 62 to 70%. Milk bread, cinnamon rolls and ciabatta are 70 to 80%. Focaccia is about 80%.
  2. Choose your enrichers. Eggs, milk, butter, sugar, honey, oil, cream or milk powder. Each card on the bread page says what it does.
  3. Enter flour and the hydration, and leave the rest blank. Blanks get recommended amounts. Water makes up the remainder.
  4. Read the warnings. They flag unusual salt, very high sugar or fat, missing yeast, or a hydration that is far outside the usual range.
  5. Change one thing and press Enter again. Changing one number at a time shows you what it does.

An example

500 g flour, 65% hydration, with egg, butter, sugar and milk, everything else blank. The calculator gives 75 g egg, 75 g butter, 50 g sugar, 185 g milk and 94 g plain water, plus 10 g salt and 10 g instant yeast. The total is about 998 g of dough, at 65% true hydration.

Those recommended amounts are chosen to sit inside the range of King Arthur's published enriched doughs: salt about 2%, instant yeast 1.4 to 2.9%, sugar 7 to 16%, butter 12 to 24% for rolls and sandwich bread.

Reading the warnings

A warning is not an error. It means the recipe is possible but unusual. Over about 20% sugar and honey slows regular yeast. Salt outside 1.5 to 3% of flour is unusual. Fat above 60% of flour needs long mixing and gentle handling. Hydration under 45% is very stiff.
