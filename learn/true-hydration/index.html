True Hydration: Why Milk, Eggs and Butter Count as Water

Hydration is the weight of water in a dough divided by the weight of flour. In a plain loaf that is just the water you pour in. In an enriched dough, much of the water arrives inside other ingredients.

A worked example

500 g flour, 100 g water, 100 g egg and 150 g milk. The water inside the egg is 100 × 0.76 = 76 g. The water inside the milk is 150 × 0.88 = 132 g. Total water: 100 + 76 + 132 = 308 g. True hydration is 308 ÷ 500 = 61.6%.

Counting only the plain water gives 100 ÷ 500 = 20%, which would suggest a very stiff dough. The real dough is a soft, workable one.

Why rich doughs read low

Butter and egg yolks add richness, not much water. A brioche is wet and soft to handle but has a true hydration of roughly 45 to 55%, because the softness comes from fat. Compare doughs by true hydration and a 62% milk bread and a 62% lean loaf will feel similar in water, though not in crumb.
