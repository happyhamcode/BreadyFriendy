Cake Balance: The Ratio Rules

A cake recipe is a balance. Flour and eggs build structure. Fat and sugar tenderize. Liquid dissolves the sugar and makes steam. Leavening lifts. Change one and the others have to follow.

Pound cake: 1:1:1:1

The classic pound cake is equal weights of flour, butter, sugar and eggs. King Arthur's pound cakes are close to it but not equal. Across five of them, butter is 78 to 100% of the flour, sugar 82 to 138% (the high end is a chocolate one) and eggs 42 to 88%. Most also add some milk or sour cream, and many a little baking powder.

Butter cakes

Across six King Arthur yellow, vanilla, birthday and fudge cakes, sugar is 83 to 165% of the flour (most are 83 to 108%), butter 24 to 63%, eggs 50 to 93%, and milk, yogurt or cream 62 to 114%. In every one the liquid (eggs plus milk or yogurt) weighs at least as much as the sugar.

High-ratio cakes go further: sugar at least equal to the flour weight and liquid at least equal to the sugar, with cake flour and an emulsifier to hold it together. The Baking Sense site describes that rule.

Sponge and chiffon

Sponge. A sponge is mostly whipped eggs. King Arthur's genoise has 352% eggs and 166% sugar against the flour, with 63% melted butter, and its sponge cake with cranberry curd has 250% eggs and 165% sugar with no butter. Neither has baking powder.

Chiffon. King Arthur's three chiffon cakes use sugar 106 to 124%, oil 41 to 43%, egg yolks 50 to 59%, egg whites 96 to 114%, and water or milk 65 to 95%, with baking powder at 4 to 5%.

Leavening

King Arthur's cakes use about ¾ to 1½ teaspoons of baking powder per cup of flour, which is 2.5 to 5% of the flour weight. Cakes leavened with baking soda alone use about 2.5%, and they have an acid to react with, such as buttermilk, yogurt or cocoa.

What the calculator checks

  • Pound cake: butter and sugar between 75 and 140% of the flour, eggs between 40 and 140%.
  • Butter cake: sugar at least 80% of the flour, and total liquid at least equal to the sugar.
  • Sponge: eggs at least 180% of the flour.
  • Chiffon: whites at least equal to yolks.
  • Every cake: fat and total leavening (baking powder plus soda) within the range of the published recipes for that type, baking soda only with an acid, and soda no higher than 3% of the flour.

The ranges are set so that every King Arthur recipe I checked passes with no warning. Treat a warning as a prompt to look, not a verdict. Other bakers' recipes will sit differently.
