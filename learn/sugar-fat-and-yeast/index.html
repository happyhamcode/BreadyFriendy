Sugar, Fat and Yeast in Enriched Dough

Sugar and fat make dough soft and sweet, and they also make it harder for yeast to do its job.

Sugar

Sugar attracts water. In a dough it pulls water away from the yeast cells, which slows them down. King Arthur recommends SAF Gold, an osmotolerant yeast that copes better with sugar, when a recipe has at least 1 tablespoon of sugar per cup of flour. That is about 10% of the flour weight.

King Arthur's own enriched recipes with regular yeast use sugar or honey from 7% (sandwich bread) through 10 to 11% (brioche, dinner rolls) and 16% (milk bread) up to 18% (honey wheat rolls). So regular yeast clearly works in that range, though it may rise more slowly. Bake by Math warns when sugar and honey together are above 20% of the flour weight, just past the highest of those recipes.

Fat

Fat coats the gluten strands, which makes the crumb tender and slows staling. In King Arthur's recipes, butter is about 12% of the flour weight in plain dinner rolls, 16 to 24% in richer rolls and sandwich bread, and 43% in brioche. King Arthur's brioche works the butter in piece by piece after the eggs and flour are mixed.

Oil is about 7% in pizza dough, 15% in challah and 17% in focaccia.
