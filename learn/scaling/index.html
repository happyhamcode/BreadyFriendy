Scaling a Recipe: By Weight, Loaves and Pan Size

Scaling is multiplication. Every ingredient is multiplied by the same factor, and the factor is the new amount divided by the old amount.

Bread: by weight or by loaves

The factor is the target dough weight divided by the current total. If your recipe makes 981 g and you want two 900 g loaves, the target is 1800 g and the factor is 1800 ÷ 981 = 1.84. Multiply every ingredient by 1.84.

Bake by Math does this for you: type a total weight, or a number of loaves and the weight of each.

A caution on yeast. The calculator scales yeast in a straight line. Very large batches are often adjusted by experience, and the right change varies with the dough and the temperature, so treat the first big batch as a test.

Cake: by pan size

Batter volume scales with the area of the pan, if the batter depth stays the same. A round pan has area π × (diameter ÷ 2)². Going from an 8 inch to a 9 inch round pan multiplies the batter by (9 ÷ 8)² = 1.27, about 27% more. A square pan has area side × side. Going from an 8 inch square (64 square inches) to a 9 × 13 inch pan (117 square inches) multiplies the batter by 1.83.

Use the same unit for both pans. Bake time and temperature change with batter depth and pan size. A rule of thumb is to check doneness a few minutes early. It is not exact, so trust a skewer or a thermometer over the clock.
