Baker's Percentage: Why Flour Is Always 100%

Baker's percentage is a way of writing a recipe where every ingredient is a percentage of the flour's weight. Flour is always 100%. That one rule makes recipes easy to compare, scale and fix.

A bread example

Take 500 g flour, 325 g water, 10 g salt and 5 g instant yeast. Divide each by the flour weight:

IngredientGramsBaker's %
Flour500100%
Water32565%
Salt102%
Instant yeast51%

The percentages do not add up to 100. They are all measured against the flour, not against the total.

Why it is useful

Scaling. To make 1.5 times as much, multiply every gram by 1.5, or just enter 750 g of flour and let the others follow. Every percentage stays the same.

Comparing. A loaf with 2% salt has 2% salt whether it uses 300 g of flour or 3 kg. Two recipes of different sizes can be compared line by line.

Fixing. If a dough is too stiff, you can see that water is 58% and change it to 65%, instead of guessing in cups.

A cake example

A classic pound cake is equal weights of flour, butter, sugar and eggs, called 1:1:1:1. In baker's percentage that is 100% flour, 100% butter, 100% sugar and 100% eggs. Modern recipes drift from it, and the percentage shows exactly how far.
